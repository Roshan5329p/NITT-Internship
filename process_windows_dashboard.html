    🔬 Numerical Reconciliations: Physical Basis of Thermal & Mechanical Discrepancies
    1. Three Coexisting Cooling Rates Explained: The dashboard reports three different cooling rates because they describe three distinct physical locations and thermodynamic regimes in the build:
    • Centerline Solidification Rate (1.17 × 10⁶ K/s): Evaluated at the trailing solidus isotherm (1,600°C) along the melt pool centerline where Ṫ = G · R = 1.71 × 10⁴ K/cm × 68.4 cm/s. This dictates the primary dendritic / cellular spacing λ₁ = 35.5 · Ṫ⁻⁰·²⁸ = 0.71 µm.
    • Bontha Root Apex Rate (4.89 × 10⁶ K/s): Evaluated at the deep root apex (z = zm) directly beneath the high-intensity beam where thermal conduction into the cold substrate creates an ultra-steep gradient G = 5.1 × 10⁴ K/cm, generating ultra-fine surface-quenched laths (0.48 µm).
    • Pyrometry Dwell Rate (1.4 × 10⁵ K/s): Evaluated across the solid-state β → α' transformation window (995°C down to 575°C martensite start) where ΔT = 420 K occurs over dwell time t_β/Ms ≈ 2.1 ms. This controls solid-state lath thickening rather than liquid solidification.
    2. Rosenthal Point Source vs. KU Leuven Hybrid Dimensions: The classical Rosenthal (1946) model assumes an infinitesimal point source with concentrated heat flux, predicting melt pool dimensions of 145 × 74 µm. The KU Leuven semi-analytical hybrid model (Coen et al. 2022) incorporates a finite Gaussian laser spot beam radius (r₀ = 40 µm) and vapor recoil depression, yielding an aspect ratio of 0.51 and melt pool dimensions of 127 × 65 µm.
    3. Tabor Hardness-Strength Scaling: The relationship between Vickers microhardness and ultimate tensile strength uses empirical calibration for additive Ti-6Al-4V: σ_UTS ≈ 2.81 × HV₀.₃ (yielding 1,034 MPa for 339 HV). The classic theoretical Tabor factor of 3.0 applies strictly to rigid plastic indentation of non-strain-hardening metals (giving ~1,017–1,040 MPa), demonstrating physical consistency with standard tensile tests.
